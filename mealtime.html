Mealtime: Meal Planning

Mealtime is a mobile meal planning and grocery management platform designed to simplify everyday food planning through an intuitive, user-centred experience. The product brings together meal planning, budgeting, grocery list management, food waste reduction, and accessibility features into a single digital workflow, helping users make faster, more informed decisions while reducing cognitive load.

THE ASK

Grocery shopping often requires users to switch between multiple disconnected tools for meal planning, budgeting, shopping lists, and pantry management. This fragmented experience increases cognitive load, creates unnecessary friction, and makes it difficult to maintain healthy habits or reduce food waste. The objective was to design a unified digital product that simplifies these complex workflows through intuitive navigation, structured information architecture, and accessible user interactions.

THE DESIGN

Mealtime was designed as an end-to-end product experience that combines meal planning, grocery management, budgeting, and accessibility into a single responsive application. The interface prioritises intuitive navigation, clear visual hierarchy, and streamlined user flows, allowing users to complete everyday planning tasks efficiently while supporting a broad range of accessibility needs.

INITIAL RESEARCH

I conducted user research to better understand existing shopping behaviours, planning habits, accessibility needs, and pain points throughout the grocery planning journey. These insights informed feature prioritisation, information architecture decisions, and the overall product strategy, ensuring the experience addressed real user needs rather than assumptions.

COMPETITOR RESEARCH

I performed a competitor analysis across leading grocery, meal planning, and navigation applications to identify strengths, usability issues, and recurring user frustrations. I also synthesised qualitative user reviews to uncover unmet needs, allowing design decisions to be grounded in both market patterns and real user behaviour.

USER JOURNEY

Research findings were translated into detailed user journeys and task flows to map key decision points throughout the shopping experience. Particular attention was given to accessibility, evaluating how existing navigation and shopping applications support users with disabilities and identifying opportunities to reduce friction through clearer navigation, simplified interactions, and inclusive design patterns.

WIREFRAMING

Low-fidelity wireframes were developed to rapidly explore information architecture, navigation patterns, and interface layouts before visual refinement. Design decisions focused on reducing interaction cost through quick-add actions, personalised filtering, streamlined shopping workflows, and features that supported users with diverse accessibility requirements.

LOW-FIDELITY PROTOTYPING

Early interactive prototypes were used to validate core interaction patterns, navigation structure, and task completion flows before progressing to higher-fidelity interfaces. Multiple concepts were explored to determine the most intuitive way to present meal planning, grocery management, community features, and in-store assistance.

USABILITY TESTING

Usability testing revealed several opportunities to simplify the user experience. Participants prioritised practical features that supported shopping efficiency over social functionality, leading to the removal of lower-value interactions and the introduction of clearer task-focused workflows.

Feedback also informed accessibility improvements, including interactive progress checklists, audio confirmation for visually impaired users, and expiry notifications to better support food management and reduce waste.

HIGH-FIDELITY PROTOTYPING

Findings from usability testing informed the final high-fidelity interface, resulting in a more intuitive navigation structure, improved visual hierarchy, and clearer interaction feedback. New interface components, including notification summaries, interactive checklists, and visual expiry indicators, improved discoverability while reducing cognitive load throughout the shopping journey.

IMPACT

The project demonstrates a complete end-to-end product design process, from research and competitor analysis through user journeys, wireframing, prototyping, usability testing, and iterative refinement. The resulting product consolidated multiple disconnected grocery planning tasks into a single user-centred workflow while introducing accessibility-focused features that supported a broader range of users.

THE TAKEAWAY

TThis project strengthened my ability to translate research into scalable product decisions and design intuitive digital experiences for complex everyday workflows. I gained experience balancing usability, accessibility, information architecture, and visual design while iterating through multiple prototype cycles based on user feedback.

It also reinforced the importance of designing flexible systems that accommodate diverse user needs rather than assuming a single user journey. Considering accessibility from the earliest design stages helped shape more inclusive interaction patterns that reduced friction and supported a wider range of users.
