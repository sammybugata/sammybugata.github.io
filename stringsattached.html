Strings Attached

An interactive physical experience in the form of a carnival game that explores the tension between human behavior and digital automation. By subverting the logic of CAPTCHA tests, the project asks: if technology defines human behavior, does deviating from it make you less human—or prove that you are?

CLIENT

ACADEMIC / RESEARCH PROJECT

ROLE

UX DESIGNER, DEVELOPER

INDUSTRY

SPECULATIVE DESIGN & INTERACTION DESIGN

OVERVIEW

During this university project, I co-designed and built an interactive physical experience that critiques how digital systems standardize and validate human identity. Inspired by the absurdity of CAPTCHA tests where humans must prove their humanity to a machine using rigid, pre-defined parameters, we designed a live, participatory game. As an experience designer, I helped translate these theoretical questions into an engaging physical installation involving a wooden swingset structure, a split flap display system, sensory inputs, and multi-user collaboration.

The final installation blended physical computing, puppetry, and spatial design to challenge participants' perceptions of automation, control, and collective behavior.

Tools: 3D Printing, Rhino 3D, C++ (Arduino & ESP32 Files), Illustrator (Poster Design & Laser Cutting Files)

THE ASK

Websites use CAPTCHA tests to make sure you are a real person, forcing you to prove your humanity to a machine based on strict, rigid rules. The problem is that these rules are defined by one specific standard that doesn't account for the messy diversity of real human behavior. We wanted to take this invisible digital barrier and turn it into a physical, real-world experience that makes people question how much digital systems actually control and restrict our actions.

THE DESIGN

We built a multi-user carnival game where participants step into a physical harness to undergo a "humanity audition." We chose Elmo as the circus mascot because he is universally known for being fun and lighthearted. This playful framing helps players let their guard down, making the eventual realization much sharper: to pass the test, they have to abandon their natural human agency and conform completely to the rigid rules of a machine, just like a puppet. To pass or fail, the player must strike a moving frame of lit panels using specific limbs, while completely dependent on two other participants who pull ropes to move the harness side-to-side.

RESEARCH

Our team began by investigating the core contradictions of digital verification: why must humans prove their nature to a machine, and can human identity truly be calculated by a single value? We researched the history of Turing tests and CAPTCHAs, identifying the irony that algorithmic systems are inherently narrow.

To turn this research into a live installation, we mapped out the psychology of user onboarding and participation. We explored how to make a system feel intentionally demanding and rigid. This led to designing a multi-layered interaction model where user feedback loop cycles were social and spatial—requiring the audience, the player, and physical handlers to interact simultaneously.

IDEATION & DEVELOPMENT

We translated our conceptual questions into a physical journey mapped across three distinct elements:

  • The Puppet Audit: Choosing Elmo as a subverted mascot who recruits humans to become automated puppets.
  • The Mechanics of Constraint: Designing a harness and swing system facing a moving vertical grid of six sensory panels. The panels lit up dynamically, requiring participants to strike them with specific limbs, mimicking the complex visual puzzles of digital CAPTCHAs.
  • The Collective Flow: Realizing that individual movement wasn't enough to represent systemic control, we introduced a cooperative dynamic. We hooked the main player to a harness controlled via ropes by two side participants, forcing a real-time communication loop to successfully navigate the system.

GOALS & KEY FEATURES

Goals

  • Externalize abstract digital surveillance and gatekeeping into a physical, high-friction environment
  • Challenge the binary concept of "pass/fail" testing by punishing perfect performance
  • Foster forced collaboration and communication among strangers to highlight collective human adaptability

Key Features

  • Subversive Scoring Logic: Scoring 15–19 hits validates the player as "human." Scoring a flawless 20/20 triggers a "fail" for being too perfect (acting like a machine), while scoring under 15 designates the user as a "puppet" fit for the circus.
  • Live Kinetic Architecture: Moving physical target panels that shift vertically to increase cognitive load and disrupt predictability.
  • Dynamic Physical Feedback Loop: A rear shadow-puppet display that translates the user’s frantic movements into a spectacle for the audience, paired with an automated split-flap display that dynamically shifts between I'm now human, I'm not human, or glitches out to I'm human if the system detects cheating.

ONBOARDING AND OFFBOARDING

The core message of the project relied entirely on the psychological journey of the participants, which we structured through clear onboarding and offboarding phases to reveal the underlying critique of systemic control.

Onboarding

  • The Pitch: Users are welcomed to the booth and told they are auditioning to either prove their humanity or showcase their puppetry skills.
  • The Contract: To participate, they must sign a physical "consent form" to join the audition.
  • The Setup: The main player is strapped into the physical harness while two handlers take control of the steering ropes.
  • The Directive: An automated, digital system voice delivers instructions, commanding the player to hit as many panels as possible.
  • The Game & Live Feedback: As the game concludes, the system delivers immediate visual and mechanical feedback. If they pass, the panels flash green and white while the split-flap display behind them flips to read I’m now human. If they fail, the panels flash purple and red, and the sign flips to I’m not human.

Offboarding

  • The Verdict: Upon stepping out of the harness, players receive a physical card stating their results. The text explains that regardless of their score, they acted as puppets because they conformed entirely to the system’s rules.
  • The Ticket: To cement the metaphor, losing players are handed a "Backstage Pass" to immediately start performing in Elmo's puppet circus, while winning players receive an "Audience Pass" to watch the next automated subjects try the test.

Post-Experience Reflection & Evaluation

To evaluate if the experience successfully landed its message, I conducted exit interviews with participants. The aim was to evaluate whether users recognized the system's manipulation and how technology conditions real-world behavior. The discussion was guided by four core questions:

  • What do you think the experience was trying to communicate?
  • Were there moments where you adjusted your behavior to match what the system allowed?
  • Did you feel less “human” or disempowered during the experience?
  • Do you think this is happening in the real world? (Technology controlling human behaviour)

The qualitative data gathered from these interviews validated our design choices, revealing how quickly participants felt the restriction of the system and how easily they connected the physical friction of the harness to their daily digital frustrations.

THE TAKEAWAY

This project deepened my expertise in speculative design and the creation of high-fidelity physical interactions. It challenged me to design experiences where friction, ambiguity, and subversion are intentional features used to provoke thought. I learned how to orchestrate complex user journeys that balance physical hardware, collaborative human mechanics, and spatial storytelling, ultimately demonstrating how design can be used as a powerful medium for systemic critique.
